editorial response

+

statement • context • reflection

+ +

+ Freedom is central to the American Dream and Texan culture. + As such, democracy, characterized by the promise of universal independence facilitated by cooperative decision-making, has become inextricably linked to the United States in political imagination. +

+ +

+ For working people, however, this ideal has never materialized as lived experience. + At home and abroad, we remain disorganized and subject to the violent whims of an antisocial oligarchy motivated exclusively by profit. + Not only does our bicameral legislature and Electoral College prioritize territorial concerns over those of constituents themselves, corporate interests openly reign supreme in their influence over public policy. +

+ +

+ Existing campaign finance laws allow candidates to receive unlimited money from powerful donors through super PACs, rendering most representatives beholden to lobbyists rather than to the people who elect them. This disenfranchisement and the exploitation that undergirds it engenders alienation, a kind of detachment from ourselves, each other, and the environment. +

+ +

+ Extremism finds its roots there, in isolation and powerlessness. Conversely, it is not extreme to build community nor to feed one’s family. It is deeply irresponsible to compare reactionaries insulated from life to common folk who desire to make real change through dialogue. Ongoing commitment to participatory self-governance as workers is what sets democratic socialists apart from both establishment Democrats and other historical or contemporary left traditions. +

+ +

+ DSA itself is a manifestation of our potential to make a difference in solidarity. Change always involves internal and external conflict; both are worthwhile and serve to combat the extremism inherent in non-relationship as status quo. Texans who support democratic socialists running for office are making wholly moderate yet highly valuable investments into our health and that of our neighbors. +
